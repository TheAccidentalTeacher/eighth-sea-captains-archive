Source-audited reconstruction

Scott remembered the design center.

The original hid a living Caribbean underneath a nearly arcade-simple foreground: find ship, identify ship, fight, board, take prize, enter port, repeat.

The research verdict

Deep campaign. Ruthlessly simple commands.

The world tracked wars, alliances, economies, governors, treasure, family, morale, age, health, reputation, and retirement. The player mostly saw them when they changed a target, price, port, reward, or decision.

Verified mechanics

Filter the findings.

Claims below are controlled by original Commodore documentation wherever possible.

core

Three-stage identification

Sails first, ship class second, colors third. Each closer look makes escape less certain.

core

One ship fights

The player chooses a flagship. That ship’s crew capacity and guns define the encounter.

core

Board to preserve value

Merchant ships may surrender after a few broadsides; expert play minimizes damage and grapples early.

campaign

Fleets are real

Captured vessels require prize crews, slow the expedition, change flagship options, and can be sold in town.

campaign

Morale creates the clock

Crew values gold per sailor. Long cruises end in desertion or mutiny unless plunder is divided.

campaign

Division resets the snowball

The captain keeps only the flagship. Crew and other ships disperse; career reputation survives.

technical

64 KB of RAM

The C64 forced the game to swap compact scenes and data through a medium of roughly 170 KB per disk side.

technical

Reusable scenes

Map, ship fight, swordfight, town menu, and land battle combine with small state variables into a whole career.

correction

No dancing in 1987

Romance existed; the ballroom action sequence arrived in the 2004 remake.

correction

Saving did exist

The Commodore Broadsheet explains a specially formatted blank save disk and town-only saving.

correction

Perplexity mixed versions

Five passes found 92 leads, but also confused C64, NES, later ports, and the remake. Primary sources overruled it.

Memory ceiling

64 KB

That is 65,536 bytes of RAM for the machine—not a few megabytes.

Disk-side ceiling

≈170 KB

A standard 1541 disk side offered 174,848 formatted bytes. Exact occupied game bytes require a verified disk-image audit.
